# Open Problems

This file is intentionally adversarial. A proposal should be discarded or revised when it fails one of these tests.

## 1. Covariance

A hand-chosen radial dependence

\[
G\to G(r)
\]

is not a fundamental theory.

**Question:** can the screening law be derived from a diffeomorphism-invariant action, nonlocal effective action, or nonperturbative definition?

## 2. Degrees of freedom

Higher-curvature theories can introduce additional propagating modes, including ghosts.

**Question:** what is the pole/constraint structure around Minkowski, cosmological, and black-hole backgrounds?

## 3. Strong coupling

Even a ghost-free linearized theory can become strongly coupled.

**Question:** where is the strong-coupling scale relative to the screening scale?

## 4. Dynamical formation

A static metric does not prove that collapse produces that metric.

**Question:** starting from regular matter data, does the system actually enter the proposed core phase?

## 5. Inner horizon

Static regular black holes generically tend to contain an inner horizon.

**Question:** are blueshift and mass-inflation effects finite after nonlinear and semiclassical backreaction?

## 6. Energy conditions

Effective repulsion may correspond to violation of classical energy conditions.

**Question:** is that violation a controlled quantum effect, an effective geometric term, or a pathology?

## 7. Hawking backreaction

A regular static geometry can be destroyed by evaporation.

**Question:** what is the self-consistent semiclassical geometry when \(\langle T_{\mu\nu}\rangle\) is included?

## 8. Information

Removing a curvature divergence does not automatically solve the black-hole information problem.

**Question:** what is the Hilbert-space factorization, or its replacement, during formation and evaporation?

## 9. Rotating black holes

Astrophysical black holes are expected to rotate.

**Question:** can screening remain regular and stable in a Kerr-like geometry without introducing closed timelike curves or ring singularities?

## 10. Universality

A viable quantum-gravity mechanism should not depend delicately on a special equation of state or one coordinate ansatz.

**Question:** which predictions are universal under changes of matter content and microscopic parameters?

## 11. Observability

A model with no distinguishable consequences risks becoming unfalsifiable.

**Question:** what robust exterior signatures survive the enormous separation between horizon scale and core scale?

## 12. Meaning of \(G_{\rm eff}\)

A running or diagnostic Newton constant is scheme- and observable-dependent.

**Question:** which gauge-invariant scattering, response, or tidal observable should operationally define "screening"?

## 13. Temperature

Hawking temperature is a global/semiclassical quantity and local temperature can be observer-dependent.

**Question:** can any proposed temperature-triggered transition be reformulated in invariant local terms?

## 14. Non-geometric phase

A non-geometric core can become a placeholder for ignorance.

**Question:** what are the microscopic degrees of freedom and the map back to semiclassical geometry?

## 15. Recovery of GR

The model must be extraordinarily close to GR at accessible curvatures.

**Question:** what suppresses corrections in solar-system, binary-pulsar, gravitational-wave, and cosmological regimes?

## Current priority

The immediate target is not to "solve quantum gravity." It is to find the simplest covariant model that survives Problems 1–5. Failure there should be treated as useful information.


## 16. Quasitopological inner horizons

Standard GR mass-inflation arguments often use null thin shells. Recent
quasitopological-gravity work finds that the relevant pure-gravity regular
black-hole solutions do not admit such null thin shells within standard
distributional theory.

**Question:** what replaces the GR thin-shell diagnostic in the actual
quasitopological field equations, and do smooth perturbations generate an
inner-horizon singularity?

Thin-shell arguments should therefore not be treated as a proof either for or
against stability in the v2 benchmark.

## 17. Background versus principal limiting curvature

A universal bound on background curvature does not automatically bound the
curvature or coefficients of physical characteristic operators.

The vector benchmark demonstrated this explicitly.

**Question:** does the local/nonlocal QTG completion satisfy a limiting-
curvature property for its *physical perturbation principal symbols and Green
functions*, not only for the background metric?
