4D Past-Signalling Architecture Search Protocol

1. Purpose

This document defines an automated research protocol for searching for candidate mechanisms capable of transmitting sender-controlled information into the past within 3+1-dimensional semiclassical gravity.

The project does not assume either of the following conclusions in advance:

1. Past-directed controllable communication is possible.
2. Past-directed controllable communication is universally impossible.

The purpose is to systematically search the specified model space, construct candidate configurations where possible, and quantitatively identify obstructions where they arise.

The primary target is not merely the existence of a wormhole, closed timelike curve, temporal correlation, or negative-energy configuration.

The primary target is a controllable causal communication channel.

⸻

2. Primary Communication Criterion

Let the future sender choose a classical bit

[
b \in {0,1}.
]

Let the past receiver measure an observable (Y).

A candidate constitutes a successful past-signalling mechanism only if it demonstrates that

[
P(Y\mid do(b=0))
\neq
P(Y\mid do(b=1)).
]

The distinction must arise from the sender’s controllable choice rather than from a pre-existing correlation, post-selection artifact, or uncontrolled boundary condition.

The candidate must also satisfy the following physical requirements:

- a self-consistent 3+1D semiclassical spacetime;
- a specified quantum state or matter source;
- a calculable renormalized stress-energy tensor;
- accounting for the stress-energy required to create and maintain the geometry;
- causal propagation from the sender’s intervention to the receiver’s measurement;
- consistency of the relevant initial and boundary data;
- stability analysis appropriate to the model;
- explicit identification of any quantum inequalities or energy-condition constraints;
- no hidden use of the desired communication result as an input assumption.

A result based only on the existence of a CTC or wormhole does not satisfy the primary criterion.

⸻

3. Architecture Search Space

The automated search should investigate, where mathematically and computationally tractable, the following classes of spacetime and matter configurations.

3.1 Wormhole geometries

- traversable wormholes
- ring wormholes
- toroidal throats
- non-spherical throats
- multi-mouth wormholes
- multiple-loop causal structures
- thin-shell wormholes
- thick-shell wormholes
- rotating wormholes
- non-static wormholes
- time-shifted wormhole mouths
- moving wormhole mouths
- configurations involving relative mouth velocity
- configurations involving multiple wormhole mouths

The search must not assume that spherical symmetry is necessary unless the candidate model explicitly imposes it.

⸻

3.2 Quantum-state configurations

Investigate, where applicable:

- vacuum states
- squeezed vacuum states
- multimode squeezed states
- anisotropic squeezed states
- coherent states
- coherent + squeezed hybrid states
- non-vacuum quantum states
- massive scalar fields
- massless scalar fields
- fermionic fields
- Maxwell fields
- conformal fields
- interacting quantum fields
- large-(N) field content
- states involving nontrivial boundaries
- Casimir configurations
- finite Casimir apparatus
- localized negative-energy pulses

For every quantum-state candidate, calculate or bound

[
\langle T_{\mu\nu}\rangle_{\rm ren}.
]

Where possible, determine whether the required stress tensor can be maintained for the required duration and spatial extent.

⸻

3.3 Matter and support structures

The search should distinguish between:

1. the quantum source responsible for the desired stress-energy;
2. ordinary positive-energy structural material;
3. exotic or anomalous support;
4. external pumping or state-preparation mechanisms.

A candidate must account for all components contributing to the total stress-energy budget.

The existence of a negative-energy quantum component is not by itself sufficient.

⸻

3.4 Geometry/state coupling

For each candidate, attempt to solve the coupled problem

[
G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}

8\pi G
\langle T_{\mu\nu}\rangle_{\rm ren}.
]

The calculation should distinguish between:

- prescribed background geometry;
- test-field approximation;
- leading-order gravitational backreaction;
- semiclassical self-consistent solution;
- fully time-dependent evolution.

A stress tensor that reproduces the required source locally but produces only a small perturbation of ordinary spacetime must not be classified as a successful wormhole-generation mechanism.

⸻

4. Search Procedure

Each candidate architecture should pass through the following pipeline.

Stage 1: Literature and model identification

Identify the closest published or analytically defined model.

Record:

- geometry;
- matter content;
- dimensionality;
- field equations;
- boundary conditions;
- topology;
- energy conditions;
- known stability results;
- known quantum inequalities;
- known dynamical evolution;
- known chronology constraints.

The source and scope of every imported assumption must be recorded.

⸻

Stage 2: Classical geometry

Construct the spacetime metric

[
g_{\mu\nu}(x)
]

and determine:

- throat structure;
- causal structure;
- geodesics;
- chronology;
- existence of CTCs where relevant;
- time orientation;
- sender-to-receiver causal relation.

A CTC or time-shift alone is not sufficient for success.

⸻

Stage 3: Quantum source

Specify the quantum state and field content.

Calculate or approximate

[
\langle T_{\mu\nu}\rangle_{\rm ren}.
]

Determine:

- local energy density;
- pressures;
- fluxes;
- anisotropy;
- integrated null energy;
- relevant quantum inequalities;
- duration of negative-energy regions;
- spatial support;
- total energy.

⸻

Stage 4: Backreaction

Insert the calculated stress tensor into the semiclassical Einstein equation.

Determine whether the proposed source actually generates or maintains the required geometry.

The following cases must be distinguished:

Local matching

The source matches the desired stress tensor at one or more points.

Approximate matching

The source reproduces some required components or ratios but not the complete source.

Global matching

The source reproduces the stress tensor throughout the relevant region.

Dynamical self-consistency

The coupled geometry and quantum state remain self-consistent under time evolution.

Only the latter two should be considered strong evidence toward a physical construction.

⸻

Stage 5: Support accounting

Include the complete apparatus wherever applicable.

For example:

[
T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm total}

T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm quantum}
+
T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm support}
+
T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm apparatus}.
]

Do not treat the desired exotic source as existing independently of the apparatus required to produce it.

Explicitly calculate the energetic and mechanical cost of:

- confinement;
- boundaries;
- struts;
- shells;
- pumping;
- state preparation;
- field containment;
- stabilization;
- synchronization.

⸻

Stage 6: Stability and evolution

Test the candidate against relevant perturbations.

Where feasible, calculate:

- linear stability;
- mode growth;
- ghost or gradient instabilities;
- collapse;
- black-hole formation;
- throat closure;
- chronology-horizon behavior;
- quantum backreaction;
- signal capture;
- runaway energy growth.

A static solution must not automatically be interpreted as a realizable dynamical device.

⸻

Stage 7: Information-channel test

Define the sender intervention explicitly.

For example,

[
do(b=0)
]

and

[
do(b=1).
]

Run the same physical configuration under both interventions.

Calculate the receiver observable distribution:

[
P_0(Y)=P(Y\mid do(b=0)),
]

[
P_1(Y)=P(Y\mid do(b=1)).
]

Test whether

[
P_0(Y)\neq P_1(Y).
]

Where numerical methods are used, specify a statistical distinguishability criterion and numerical error budget.

The analysis must distinguish:

- genuine causal dependence;
- ordinary correlation;
- entanglement correlation;
- post-selection;
- boundary-condition dependence;
- numerical artifacts;
- instability-induced differences.

Only sender-controlled causal dependence qualifies as successful communication.

⸻

5. Classification

Every candidate must receive exactly one primary status.

A: Constructive candidate

The candidate demonstrates

[
P(Y\mid do(b=0))
\neq
P(Y\mid do(b=1))
]

within a self-consistent 3+1D semiclassical model, with the relevant geometry, quantum state, stress-energy accounting, and causal channel explicitly constructed.

This is a major result and requires unusually strong verification.

A candidate must not receive status A merely because:

- a wormhole exists;
- a CTC exists;
- negative energy exists;
- a time shift exists;
- two regions are correlated;
- a signal propagates in a prescribed background.

⸻

B: Conditional escape

The candidate achieves or approaches the communication criterion only under additional assumptions that are:

- unverified;
- experimentally unavailable;
- mathematically unproven;
- physically speculative;
- outside the presently established model;
- dependent on an unresolved consistency condition.

Examples include a required quantum state whose preparation is unknown, a special topology, an unverified stability condition, or a source whose semiclassical existence has not been established.

The unresolved assumption must be explicitly listed.

⸻

C: Obstruction

The candidate fails within the specified model because of a demonstrated mathematical, numerical, causal, energetic, quantum, or dynamical obstruction.

The obstruction must be stated with its scope.

For example:

C: finite Casimir apparatus under specified support assumptions.

is valid.

The following is not valid without a substantially broader theorem:

C: Casimir mechanisms are impossible.

The project should treat obstruction results as progress because they reduce the viable search space.

⸻

6. Scope Discipline

Every negative result must state exactly which assumptions produced it.

The automated system must distinguish:

[
\text{model-specific obstruction}
]

from

[
\text{general impossibility theorem}.
]

It must not promote the former into the latter.

Likewise, a constructive result in a toy model must not automatically be promoted to a result in full 3+1D semiclassical gravity.

⸻

7. Automated Agent Strategy

The search may be performed by an AI-driven research agent.

The agent should be allowed to:

- generate candidate geometries;
- modify ansätze;
- search literature;
- derive symbolic expressions;
- generate numerical experiments;
- write Python code;
- run parameter sweeps;
- perform perturbation analysis;
- compare alternative quantum states;
- attempt to falsify its own candidates;
- identify hidden assumptions;
- propose relaxations of failed assumptions.

The agent must not silently relax physical assumptions.

Every change to:

- dimensionality;
- topology;
- boundary conditions;
- field content;
- energy conditions;
- symmetry;
- quantum state;
- coupling;
- approximation order;
- initial data

must be recorded.

⸻

8. Adversarial Verification

For every apparent A or B result, spawn an independent verification task.

The verifier should attempt to find:

1. an algebraic error;
2. a numerical instability;
3. an inconsistent boundary condition;
4. an implicit exotic source;
5. an energy-accounting omission;
6. a violation of the assumed approximation;
7. a hidden post-selection;
8. a failure of causal controllability;
9. a distinction between correlation and intervention;
10. a collapse or chronology-protection mechanism omitted by the candidate.

A candidate should survive independent attempts at falsification before being promoted.

⸻

9. GitHub Actions Integration

GitHub Actions should function as the reproducibility and orchestration layer.

A typical workflow is:

candidate generation
        ↓
literature/model audit
        ↓
symbolic derivation
        ↓
numerical calculation
        ↓
Einstein/backreaction check
        ↓
support-energy accounting
        ↓
stability check
        ↓
do(0) / do(1) experiment
        ↓
classification
        ↓
independent verification
        ↓
artifact/report generation

Actions should automatically:

- install pinned dependencies;
- run symbolic checks;
- run numerical tests;
- execute parameter sweeps;
- run existing repository tests;
- generate machine-readable candidate records;
- generate Markdown reports;
- archive numerical outputs;
- record commit hashes;
- record model assumptions;
- reject reports lacking required fields.

The workflow should not automatically merge a candidate classified as A.

Human review should remain mandatory for any purported constructive result.

11. Search Philosophy

The system must not optimize for finding a positive result.

It should optimize for accurately mapping the space of physically and mathematically viable constructions.

A failed candidate is useful if it identifies a new obstruction.

A successful candidate is useful only if the success survives the full physical consistency checks.

The desired output is therefore not simply:

“Can time travel work?”

but a progressively refined map:

candidate space
     ↓
known obstruction
     ↓
remaining parameter space
     ↓
conditional escape
     ↓
new obstruction
     ↓
remaining parameter space
     ↓
constructive candidate

The central invariant is:

[
\boxed{
P(Y\mid do(b=0))
\neq
P(Y\mid do(b=1))
}
]

under a self-consistent 3+1D semiclassical gravitational model.

Anything weaker should be reported as an intermediate result rather than a completed past-signalling apparatus.

⸻

12. Non-Goals

This protocol does not claim:

- that past signalling is possible;
- that past signalling is impossible;
- that any particular wormhole model is physically realizable;
- that a numerical result constitutes experimental evidence;
- that CI verification constitutes peer review;
- that a successful toy model constitutes a realizable machine.

The repository should preserve these distinctions throughout the search.
