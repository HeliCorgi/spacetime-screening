# Motivation

## The problem

Classical general relativity predicts black-hole solutions whose interior evolution can terminate at a curvature singularity. In Schwarzschild spacetime,

\[
\mathcal K
=
R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
=
\frac{48G_N^2M^2}{r^6},
\]

so the classical curvature invariant diverges as \(r\to0\).

A singularity is not merely "a place with a very strong force." In the singularity theorems, the central diagnostic is geodesic incompleteness under specified assumptions. From the perspective of effective field theory, a curvature divergence is also a warning that the classical variables are being extrapolated beyond the regime where they can be trusted.

## Working intuition

The project explores a negative-feedback alternative:

\[
\text{compression}
\rightarrow
\text{higher invariant scale}
\rightarrow
\text{modified gravitational response}
\rightarrow
\text{suppressed further curvature growth}.
\]

This is deliberately broader than saying "Newton's constant depends on radius." Radius is coordinate-dependent and cannot be the fundamental trigger.

Possible covariant triggers include:

- local curvature invariants;
- eigenvalues of tidal-curvature operators;
- invariant matter densities;
- a renormalization-group scale determined dynamically from the state.

## Why screening?

A high-energy weakening of a dimensionful Newton coupling is not unprecedented. In asymptotic-safety scenarios, the dimensionless Newton coupling can approach a non-Gaussian fixed point while

\[
G(k)\sim \frac{g_*}{k^2}
\]

at large \(k\).

This does not establish the spacetime-screening hypothesis, but it demonstrates that "higher energy means a larger dimensionful \(G\)" is not mandatory.

## Why a phase transition?

A simple finite-curvature regular core can remain entirely geometrical. But a stronger hypothesis is that the metric itself is emergent and fails as a complete variable in the deepest quantum region.

This possibility becomes relevant when one tries to demand both:

- ordinary weak-curvature GR in the asymptotic region; and
- a qualitatively different weak-curvature or nearly flat state at the black-hole center.

A new order parameter or branch label can distinguish two states that have similar local curvature but different microscopic organization.

## Research stance

The repository treats the following statements differently:

**Known context:** regular black holes, RG running, higher-curvature gravity, holographic spacetime emergence, and inner-horizon instability are established research subjects.

**Project hypothesis:** these ingredients may fit into a common description in which high curvature triggers gravitational screening and, possibly, a transition out of the geometric phase.

The purpose of the repository is to find where that hypothesis fails or becomes mathematically unavoidable.
