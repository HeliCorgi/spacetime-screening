# Generalized-Proca Mapping and Odd-Parity Warning

The current two-vector benchmark is a special bi-generalized-Proca theory.
This note makes the mapping explicit and compares its structure with the
existing odd-parity stability literature.

Reference for generalized-Proca odd modes:

R. Kase, M. Minamitsuji, S. Tsujikawa, Y.-L. Zhang,
*Black hole perturbations in vector-tensor theories: The odd-mode analysis*,
JCAP **02** (2018) 048,
https://doi.org/10.1088/1475-7516/2018/02/048

## 1. Mapping of one vector sector

Define

\[
X=-\frac12W_\mu W^\mu.
\]

The interaction is

\[
\mathcal L[W]
=
4G^{\mu\nu}W_\mu W_\nu
+
8W^2\nabla_\mu W^\mu
+
6(W^2)^2.
\]

Since

\[
W^2=-2X,
\]

the algebraic and cubic pieces become

\[
8W^2\nabla\cdot W
=
-16X\nabla\cdot W,
\]

and

\[
6(W^2)^2=24X^2.
\]

Thus, apart from the overall \(\ell^2\) and action normalization, the
generalized-Proca functions have the structure

\[
\boxed{
G_2(X)=24X^2,
\qquad
G_3(X)=-16X
}
\]

for the \(A\) sector.

The Einstein-tensor coupling is the quartic generalized-Proca interaction
generated by a \(G_4(X)\) linear in \(X\):

\[
\boxed{
\Delta G_4(X)\propto 4X
}.
\]

The \(B\) sector enters the action with the opposite sign, so its interaction
functions carry the opposite signs.

## 2. Null background does not remove the quartic derivative coupling

On the exact black-hole background,

\[
A^2=B^2=0,
\]

and therefore

\[
X_A=X_B=0.
\]

However,

\[
G_{4,X}\neq0.
\]

Thus the quartic derivative structure remains active even though the
background norm is zero.

This matters because odd-parity stability is controlled not only by the value
of \(G_4\), but also by its derivatives and by the radial/longitudinal
background vector.

## 3. Longitudinal components in static coordinates

The exact solution is naturally regular in ingoing coordinates,

\[
A=a(r)dv,
\qquad
B=b(r)dv.
\]

Transforming to static coordinates,

\[
dv=dt+\frac{dr}{f},
\]

gives

\[
A_t=a(r),
\qquad
A_r=\frac{a(r)}{f(r)},
\]

and

\[
B_t=b(r),
\qquad
B_r=\frac{b(r)}{f(r)}.
\]

Both vectors remain null:

\[
-\frac{A_t^2}{f}+fA_r^2=0,
\]

and similarly for \(B\).

The coordinate divergence of \(A_r,B_r\) at a horizon is not itself a physical
singularity; the ingoing one-forms are regular. What is invariantly relevant
for comparison with generalized-Proca branches is that the backgrounds possess
a nonzero longitudinal/radial component.

## 4. At least one horizon vector profile is always nonzero

On the regular branch \(q_b=-q_a=-q\), the ingoing profiles at a horizon
\(f(r_h)=0\) are

\[
a_h
=
\frac{r_h-q/2}{2r_h^2},
\]

\[
b_h
=
\frac{r_h+q/2}{2r_h^2}.
\]

Their sum is

\[
\boxed{
a_h+b_h=\frac1{r_h}
}.
\]

Therefore the two horizon profiles cannot vanish simultaneously.

For

\[
q>0,\qquad r_h>0,
\]

we have

\[
\boxed{
b_h>0
}.
\]

Hence at least one vector necessarily carries a nonzero longitudinal profile
at every positive-radius horizon.

## 5. Fully extremal branch

For

\[
r_h=\frac{4M}{3},
\]

the \(A\)-profile can be made to vanish at the horizon only by choosing

\[
q=\frac{8M}{3}.
\]

But then

\[
\boxed{
b_h=\frac{3}{4M}\neq0.
}
\]

So no parameter choice can place both vector sectors on a vanishing-
longitudinal branch simultaneously.

## 6. Comparison with known odd-parity instabilities

Kase et al. studied odd-parity perturbations of generalized-Proca black holes
and found a particularly relevant pattern:

- cubic \(G_3(X)\) interactions alone do not introduce a new odd-parity
  stability condition relative to GR;
- quartic \(G_4(X)\) interactions linear or power-law in \(X\), corresponding
  to Einstein-tensor-type vector couplings, can produce ghost or Laplacian
  instabilities on branches with nonzero longitudinal \(A_1\);
- branches with \(A_1=0\) can avoid that specific pathology for suitable
  parameters.

This makes the present benchmark structurally close to a known dangerous
class:

\[
\boxed{
G_{4,X}\neq0
\quad+\quad
\text{nonzero longitudinal vector}.
}
\]

## 7. Why this is not yet a proof of instability here

The published single-vector result cannot simply be copied onto this model.

Important differences are:

1. there are two vector sectors;
2. their actions enter with opposite signs;
3. both background vectors are null;
4. there is no ordinary Maxwell kinetic term;
5. the axial vector perturbations are algebraic auxiliaries;
6. the exact regular metric is not the stealth-Schwarzschild solution used in
   the clearest known instability example.

The two auxiliary sectors can modify or cancel the dangerous contribution
after they are eliminated.

Therefore the existing literature supplies a **red flag**, not a verdict.

## 8. Stronger statement from the current calculation

The repository now establishes two facts simultaneously:

\[
\boxed{
\text{axial vector modes are auxiliary}
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\text{at least one longitudinal background is unavoidable at the horizon}.
}
\]

Hence the only decisive calculation is the reduced metric action obtained
after solving both auxiliary vector constraints.

If the reduced Regge-Wheeler kinetic coefficient changes sign, the benchmark
is ruled out as a stable screening theory.

If it remains positive, the two-vector constrained structure has evaded a
pathology that affects simpler single-vector generalized-Proca branches.
