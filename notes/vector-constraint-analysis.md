# Vector Constraints, Radial Stability, and Strong-Coupling Diagnostics

This note examines the two-vector action used as the current action-level
benchmark for Spacetime Screening.

Reference:

A. Eichhorn and P. G. S. Fernandes,
*Regular black holes without mass-inflation instability and gravastars from
modified gravity*, Phys. Rev. D **113**, L081501 (2026),
arXiv:2508.00686,
https://doi.org/10.1103/nqz2-88zf

The purpose here is to separate three logically different statements:

1. the black-hole solution is regular;
2. radial linear perturbations are constrained and stable;
3. the full theory has a healthy perturbative spectrum.

The first two have substantial support. The third is not yet established.

## 1. Vector action

For one Weyl vector,

\[
\mathcal L[W]
=
4G^{\mu\nu}W_\mu W_\nu
+
8W^2\nabla_\mu W^\mu
+
6(W^2)^2.
\]

The two-vector theory uses

\[
S
=
\frac1{16\pi}
\int d^4x\sqrt{-g}
\left[
R+\ell^2\left(\mathcal L[A]-\mathcal L[B]\right)
\right].
\]

The published paper explicitly notes that neither vector has a conventional
kinetic term and interprets them as nonlinear constraints.

## 2. Published radial perturbation result

The Supplemental Material perturbs

\[
ds^2
=
-\left(f+\epsilon F_1\right)dt^2
+
\left(f^{-1}+\epsilon F_2\right)dr^2
+
r^2d\Omega^2,
\]

together with radial perturbations of both vectors.

After Fourier transformation in time, the vector equations constrain the
vector perturbations in terms of \(F_1,F_2\). The remaining independent
equations permit only

\[
\widetilde F_1=c_1 f,
\qquad
\widetilde F_2=0.
\]

The \(c_1\) mode is removable by a temporal-coordinate redefinition. Thus the
radial metric perturbations vanish, and so do the radial vector perturbations.

Within this sector the black holes are therefore linearly stable.

This is stronger than simply checking a positive potential: there is no
independent radial propagating mode at linear order.

The paper itself emphasizes that non-radial quasi-normal-mode and stability
analyses remain future work.

## 3. Quadratic spectrum around asymptotic Minkowski space

The regular black-hole vector profiles fall as

\[
A_\mu,B_\mu=O(r^{-2}),
\]

so the relevant asymptotic vacuum is

\[
g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu},
\qquad
A_\mu=B_\mu=0.
\]

Expand

\[
g_{\mu\nu}
=
\eta_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu},
\qquad
W_\mu=w_\mu.
\]

Order counting gives

\[
G^{\mu\nu}W_\mu W_\nu
=
O(hw^2),
\]

\[
W^2\partial_\mu W^\mu
=
O(w^3),
\]

and

\[
(W^2)^2=O(w^4).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
S^{(2)}_{\rm vector}=0
}
\]

around the exact asymptotic vacuum.

At quadratic order the action contains the ordinary Einstein-Hilbert graviton
sector, but no independent vector quadratic operator.

This means:

- there is no asymptotic vector ghost visible at quadratic order;
- there is also no ordinary asymptotic vector propagator;
- the primary hair is not generated by a conventional weakly coupled vector
  particle excitation around Minkowski space.

## 4. Why this raises a strong-coupling question

For a conventional perturbative field theory, the quadratic action defines
the propagator and the interaction terms are treated as corrections.

Here the vector expansion around \(W=0\) starts with

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_{\rm vector}
=
8\ell^2w^2\,\partial\cdot w
+
6\ell^2(w^2)^2
+\cdots
}
\]

with no \(w^2\) term.

Thus there is no canonical normalization of an independent vector wave around
the asymptotic vacuum.

There are two qualitatively different interpretations:

### Constraint interpretation

The vectors are genuinely auxiliary/constrained variables and are never meant
to define independent asymptotic particles. Then the absence of a propagator
is intentional.

### Strong-coupling interpretation

If some vector degree of freedom is actually dynamical after the complete
metric-vector constraint analysis, the vanishing quadratic term means that
its linear kinetic operator degenerates and nonlinear terms control the
dynamics arbitrarily close to the vacuum. This is the standard signature of a
strong-coupling problem.

The current calculation does not choose between these interpretations.

A full Dirac-Hamiltonian constraint analysis is needed.

## 5. Constant null background

The black-hole vectors are null on shell. To understand the local structure,
consider a constant null background

\[
W^\mu=\bar W^\mu+w^\mu,
\qquad
\bar W^2=0.
\]

On fixed Minkowski space, the quadratic vector Lagrangian becomes

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_2
=
16(\bar W\cdot w)\,\partial_\mu w^\mu
+
24(\bar W\cdot w)^2
}.
\]

It is first order in derivatives.

The Hessian with respect to all four velocities is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\partial^2\mathcal L_2}
{\partial\dot w^\mu\partial\dot w^\nu}
=
0
}.
\]

Thus even on a nonzero null background there is no ordinary second-order
vector kinetic matrix.

## 6. Principal symbol

The fixed-metric flat-space vector equation is

\[
E^\mu
=
3W^2W^\mu
+
2W^\mu\partial_\alpha W^\alpha
-
\partial^\mu(W^2)
=
0.
\]

Linearizing around constant null \(\bar W^\mu\), the principal symbol for
Fourier covector \(k_\mu\) is

\[
\boxed{
P^\mu{}_\nu(k)
=
2\left(
\bar W^\mu k_\nu
-
k^\mu\bar W_\nu
\right)
}.
\]

Because this is the difference of two rank-one matrices,

\[
\boxed{
\det P=0
}
\]

for every \(k_\mu\).

All \(3\times3\) minors vanish, so

\[
\operatorname{rank}P\le2.
\]

For generic \(k\) not parallel to \(\bar W\),

\[
\operatorname{rank}P=2.
\]

If

\[
k^\mu\parallel\bar W^\mu,
\]

the symbol vanishes entirely.

This is a highly degenerate principal part. It is consistent with a
constraint system, but it is not the principal symbol of an ordinary
hyperbolic Proca wave equation.

## 7. Relation to generalized Proca

The Weyl-Gauss-Bonnet construction belongs to the generalized-Proca family,
whose special derivative structure is designed to avoid an Ostrogradsky
degree of freedom.

That fact is relevant but not sufficient here.

"Free of Ostrogradsky higher-derivative ghosts" does not by itself establish:

- a nondegenerate kinetic matrix on every background;
- positivity of all physical kinetic eigenvalues;
- hyperbolicity;
- absence of strong coupling;
- stability of every non-spherical black-hole perturbation.

The two-vector model is especially degenerate because the conventional vector
kinetic term is absent.

## 8. Exact regularity surface

For the general two-hair solution,

\[
f(r)
=
1-
\frac{
8Mr^3+\ell^2(q_a^2-q_b^2)
}{
4r\left[r^3+(q_a-q_b)\ell^2\right]
}.
\]

Regularity requires

\[
q_b=-q_a.
\]

Our symbolic expansion finds that off this surface,

\[
\boxed{
K
\sim
\frac{3(q_a+q_b)^2}{4r^6}
}
\qquad(r\to0).
\]

Thus regularity is an exact codimension-one condition in the static solution
space.

A tiny static mismatch

\[
\delta q=q_a+q_b
\]

restores a Schwarzschild-like \(r^{-6}\) curvature divergence.

This is **not** by itself a dynamical instability. The hair parameters are
integration constants, and the radial perturbation analysis indicates that
time-dependent spherical perturbations are constrained.

However, it makes the following dynamical question essential:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Does evolution preserve }q_a+q_b=0?
}
\]

That must be answered in collapse simulations or by identifying a conserved
constraint/charge enforcing the regular branch.

## 9. Zero-frequency caveat

The published radial calculation is performed after Fourier transformation
and uses the real and imaginary parts of the frequency-space vector
equations.

Static deformations that move between neighboring exact solutions correspond
to an \(\omega=0\) sector and should be audited separately before being
identified with dynamical perturbations.

This does not contradict the published radial stability result. It simply
separates:

- time-dependent radial stability;
- static motion in the space of integration constants;
- full non-spherical stability.

## 10. Current status

The evidence currently supports:

\[
\boxed{
\text{regular exact solution}
+
\text{no radial linear instability}
}
\]

but not yet

\[
\boxed{
\text{fully healthy quantum/perturbative theory}.
}
\]

The next decisive calculations are:

1. full ADM/Dirac constraint counting for the two-vector action;
2. odd-parity non-spherical perturbations;
3. even-parity non-spherical perturbations;
4. characteristic/hyperbolicity analysis;
5. cubic strong-coupling scale after solving constraints;
6. dynamical preservation of the regularity condition.
